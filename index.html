Jeff Hanna

I pick at a problem until it tells me why.

I got my start diagnosing electrical and drivability problems in an automotive shop, and security turned out to be the same work on different machines: form a theory, test it, and keep asking why until you understand it.

I spent four years at U.S. Cyber Command, where I supervised a branch in the Intelligence Directorate and built the command's first OSINT program. At GoDaddy I spent four and a half years as a Senior Security Engineer, building threat intelligence programs, LLM-orchestrated intel pipelines and credential remediation automation that didn't exist before I got there. Now I'm the founding threat intelligence hire on a security engineering team, building the CTI program from zero and securing the AI gateway that model and MCP tool traffic runs through.

At home I keep a lab where I tinker with whatever has my interest: a SIEM and the log pipelines that feed it, network and packet capture analysis, Linux administration, a GitLab instance for practicing CI security, and AI systems I test and try to break, including my own LLM proxy. I also build the tools I want to use, like custom agent harnesses, a terminal UI and a multiplexer. I'm curious by default. The problems I like most are the ones I haven't figured out yet, and I stay with them until I have. I'm also always open to learning from people, because every one of them knows something I don't.
